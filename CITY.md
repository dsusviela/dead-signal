## Vision

The city must feel like a continuous, recently quarantined city rather than a road grid with
isolated landmarks. It needs fire and police services, parks, a supermarket, parking, a
hospital, a graveyard, dense ordinary fabric, a strongly industrial Ashworks, and a military
containment complex around Patient Furnace. The named buildings must be enterable and
lootable. The full map must tell the player what category of supply a location offers, while
the exact contents and placement vary by run.

Exploration remains open. Players may scout any reachable district and backtrack freely.
The campaign has practical dependencies, but the HUD must not turn them into a constant
linear waypoint trail. Architecture, signage, light, paper, and the map reveal the chain.

### Non-negotiable invariants

- **City geometry is fixed across run seeds.** Buildings, lots, doors, fences, route
  reservations, debris, and the four possible bulldozer pads are authored or generated by
  the fixed geometry stream. The fire station's reserved fire-truck slot is fixed too.
- **Run variation is state, not city geometry.** Loot contents and positions, vehicle fuel,
  and the one active bulldozer pad may vary with the run seed.
- **Every required location is guaranteed.** Weighted rolls are only for ordinary urban
  fabric and optional secondary venues.
- **The Northline fire department has one guaranteed driveable fire truck.** Its apparatus
  bay/apron slot is fixed; fuel may vary by run. It is optional transport, not a campaign key.
- **Every main route remains traversable on foot.** The bulldozer opens useful shortcuts and
  optional access; it never repairs an otherwise impossible base layout.
- **All requested enclosed civic locations are enterable.** Parks, graveyards, parking lots,
  and industrial yards are deliberately roofless locations, not failed buildings.
- **Location identity belongs to a location/building record, not to a wall obstacle.** A
  building may have many wall, furniture, and fence obstacles.
- **Day 9 remains visible.** No overgrowth or long-term ruin; failures, ash, fresh demolition,
  smoke damage, and abandoned work tell the story.
- **No government rescue arrives.** A radio reply may confirm that another civilian group is
  alive, but the protagonists restore power, recover the override, open Checkpoint Nine, and
  conduct their own escape.

## Canon decisions and resolved inconsistencies

These decisions supersede older wording in `lore.md`, the HUD, and the README. Updating those
surfaces is tracked below.

1. **Story landmarks and urban anchors are different layers.** Checkpoint Nine, St. Orison,
   Blackglass Radio, Furnace Plant, the Collapsed Quarter, and Patient Furnace remain the
   story landmarks. The supermarket, hospital campus, radio station, machine-shop compound,
   graveyard/chapel, and quarantine facility are the urban anchors used to shape blocks and
   loot. One does not replace the other.
2. **The campaign dependency is South Blocks → Ashworks → chapel → hospital → quarantine →
   Northline → Checkpoint Nine.** Exploration around that chain is open. Fuel restores the
   chapel generator and emergency circuit; the hospital supplies the context and records;
   quarantine yields the command payload and gate override; Blackglass sends the evidence and
   distress call; the squad then opens the gate and escapes without a rescue force.
3. **Patient Furnace is a local attractor, not the origin of the worldwide infestation.**
   Survivor scent still creates permanent run pressure. Patient Furnace amplifies the pull
   toward this city; killing it reduces the local convergence but does not cure the outbreak
   or erase accumulated danger.
4. **A reply is not a rescue.** Another civilian voice may answer the final transmission and
   coordinate outside Checkpoint Nine, but nobody arrives to fight, supply the squad, or open
   the gate for them.
5. **The supermarket can contain provisions without looking untouched.** Public shelves were
   stripped during the first nine days. Randomized stock survives in a locked back room,
   loading cage, damaged vending stock, and overlooked pockets. This supports food/energy loot
   without contradicting the scarcity premise.
6. **The chapel generator is a real campaign dependency.** It restores the emergency circuit
   used by Blackglass and Checkpoint Nine. It is not merely decorative and must consume a
   distinct vehicle/generator fuel resource.
7. **Flamethrower fuel and machine fuel are separate resources.** The current `ammo.fuel`
   remains incendiary fuel. Generators, cars, and the bulldozer use vehicle fuel; implementing
   refuelling is part of this revamp.
8. **“Enterable by construction” applies only to the existing one-room shell.** The current
   wall gap, roof fade, and nav stamping are reusable foundations. Hospitals, supermarkets,
   stations, and industrial buildings still require multi-room layouts, interior doors,
   furniture lanes, and validation.
9. **Map-cleared state must be stored explicitly.** Taken loot is removed from live state, so
   it cannot be reconstructed reliably by searching the remaining loot array.
10. **The seeded bulldozer does not violate fixed geometry.** All four clear pads are part of
    the fixed city; `game.js` chooses which pad receives the live vehicle for a run.
11. **The fire truck extends the existing vehicle system.** It carries up to four survivors,
    uses vehicle fuel, and has its own footprint and handling. Its first version is transport;
    hoses, water tanks, working ladders, firefighting, and a player-operated siren are deferred
    design options, not requirements hidden inside the vehicle task.
12. **Doors are openings unless explicitly interactive.** Ordinary exterior/interior doors
    begin open. Secured stockrooms, police storage, and campaign gates need explicit access
    rules and visual states; a locked-looking asset must not silently block required loot.

Every other system must consume the same ids.

| District | Canonical id | Story landmark | Urban anchor | Required supporting locations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| South Blocks | `checkpoint` | Checkpoint Nine | Crossroads supermarket | police station, market parking/loading, residential park, homes and small shops |
| Civic Ward | `hospital` | St. Orison Hospital | hospital campus | pharmacy, clinic, morgue/service building, ambulance yard |
| Northline | `northline` | Blackglass Radio | broadcast station and tower | fire station with fire truck, municipal depot, utility yard, park |
| Ashworks | `industry` | Furnace Plant | machine-shop compound | warehouse, loading yard, fuel store, machinery yard |

District boundaries are gradients in frontage, density, street width, surface clutter,
lighting, and condition. They are not hard visual color bands.

### Location relationships

- South Blocks' supermarket faces a crossroads. Parking sits beside it, loading space behind
  it, and smaller commerce leads toward it. The police station is close to the evacuation
  route but does not crowd the market intersection. The park marks the residential edge.
- Civic Ward reads as a campus: public hospital entrance, ambulance/service access, treatment
  wings, pharmacy, morgue, and staff/service spaces share a coherent perimeter.
- Northline grows quieter and more restricted toward Blackglass. The fire station comes first,
  with its fire truck visible in an apparatus bay or on the apron; then utilities and
  maintenance, then the fenced antenna/generator yard and station. Reserve truck turning and
  boarding space without blocking the station's pedestrian entrance.
- Ashworks shares one service landscape: dispatch office, machine shop, warehouse, loading
  apron, conveyors, pipe banks, spoil heaps, and crane bases. The four bulldozer pads are
  readable staging points inside that compound.
- The Old Quarter graveyard is paired with its chapel. Refuge notices, bedrolls, supplies, and
  generator paperwork explain why people gathered there and why the emergency circuit matters.
- Central Quarantine combines clinical holding, military processing, command paperwork, and
  industrial disposal. The outdoor burn yard and inner fence are part of the complex rather
  than a detached circular boss room.

## Data contract to implement

Use three layers instead of asking collision obstacles to do every job:

```js
// One semantic destination shown on the map. A compound may own several buildings/lots.
location = {
  id, kind, districtId, name, rect,
  buildingIds: [], lotIds: [],
  siteIds: [], lootProfiles: [], mapIcon, mapColor,
  required, discoveryRule
}

// One enterable structure. Existing `w.houses` can remain as a temporary alias during migration.
building = {
  id, locationId, archetypeId, rect,
  exteriorDoors: [], interiorDoors: [], rooms: [],
  lootSockets: [], roofZones: [], style
}

// Collision remains granular and refers back to its owner.
obstacle = { x, y, w, h, type, buildingId?, locationId?, ... }

// Roofless civic/industrial space with entrances and authored prop zones.
lot = { id, locationId, kind, rect, entrances: [], propZones: [], vehicleSlots: [] }

// A seeded contents container linked to a stable semantic place.
site = { id, locationId, buildingId?, lotId?, lootProfile, sockets: [], loot: [] }

// Fixed authored slot; live vehicle selection and fuel belong to game state.
vehicleSlot = { id, locationId, lotId?, buildingId?, vehicleType, rect, heading, clearance }

// Run state keyed by locationId/siteId, separate from geometry and shared by the local squad.
locationState = { discovered: false, visited: false, cleared: false }
siteState = { remainingItemIds: [], collectedItemIds: [] }
vehicle = { id, slotId, vehicleType, fuel, maxFuel, integrity, driver, riders: [], ... }
```

All world-generated pickups carry `siteId`, `locationId`, and `socketId`. Multiple site
profiles let the fire station offer medical supplies and vehicle fuel without treating its
walls as separate places. `cleared` means authored supply sites are exhausted, not that
enemies are dead or an objective is complete. Empty/evidence-only locations need explicit
map treatment; dropped weapons, enemy drops, and later rewards must not reopen a cleared
cache. Persist these states for the current run after loot compaction; cross-session saves
are not a new requirement.

`DISTRICTS` owns names, ids, bounds/classification, colors, roles, themes, map labels, ordinary
archetype weights, and required location ids. `ARCHETYPES` owns size bands, shell/interior
template, door/sign family, loot profile, map icon, and placement needs. Story landmarks refer
to locations instead of duplicating their own district facts.
