# Dead Signal — Lore Bible

Canon reference for writing, art, and objective design. If the game and this
document disagree, one of them is a bug. Open items are listed at the bottom.

## Premise

A government program ran trials on civilians in this city — the kind with a
file number, a consent form nobody read, and a disposal line item. It got out.
Within days the city was overrun and the quarantine closed around it rather
than in front of it: the people inside were not being protected, they were
being contained.

Nine days later, four civilians who have been foraging for a group that cannot
forage for itself are trying to get everyone to the gate at Checkpoint Nine.
They cannot open it yet: the emergency circuit is dead and the quarantine
override is locked in Central Command. Nobody is coming to operate either one
for them. They have to restore the circuit, recover the override, warn whoever
is still listening, and open the gate themselves.

## Timeline

**Day 9.** Specific on purpose — it sets what the art depicts:

| | State on Day 9 |
|---|---|
| Bodies | Still recognizable as people. Critical for the Slagmaw. |
| Fires | Out and cold everywhere except Ashworks, which still smolders. |
| Power | Grid failing, not gone — some streetlamps and floodlights still run. This is the in-fiction reason the lighting system works. |
| Paper | Quarantine notices, evacuation orders, and burn manifests are fresh and legible. Primary storytelling surface. |
| Decay | No overgrowth. No rust. Nine days is not long. |
| Food | Public shelves and most homes are stripped. Small reserves remain behind locked stockroom doors, in refuge stores, vending stock, and overlooked pockets. This is why the survivors are outside. |

Nobody left sooner because for nine days they believed help was coming. It
wasn't. That is the emotional baseline of the whole game.

## The survivors

**Civilians.** Not soldiers, not scientists, not police. Consequences:

- They never explain the outbreak competently. They infer, and sometimes wrong.
- Every piece of lore reaches the player through environment, not exposition.
- They use salvaged weapons badly. The pistol is a backup because it is somebody's
  glovebox gun, not a sidearm.
- They name things by what they read on signs. See *Patient Furnace* below.
- Register: frightened people improvising, under obligation to others.

They are not alone and not self-interested. They are the ones still able to
walk, sent out by a group that is holed up somewhere and waiting. Every run
into the city is an expedition in the plainest sense of the word: go out, find
what the others need, come back. This is why they carry medkits they are not
using and why the escape matters to more people than the four on screen.

## The infected

The scent is the spine of the fiction. Everything escalating in this game
escalates because the city can smell the last living people in it.

**Two pressures, and only one of them is survivable:**

- **Noise** — local, immediate, recoverable. A shot turns heads nearby; they
  investigate, search, and go back to roaming. Go quiet and you are forgotten.
- **Scent** — global, cumulative, permanent. It does not decay and there is no
  action that removes it. This is why wave pressure climbs with elapsed time
  and why the herd migrations arrive unannounced. The city is not spawning more
  infected; it is **converging**.

Patient Furnace amplifies that convergence around this city. It is an attractor,
not the source of the worldwide infestation: killing it reduces the local pull,
but it does not cure the outbreak, erase the squad's accumulated scent, or make
the streets safe.

The player should feel that noise is a mistake they made and scent is a debt
they accrue just by being alive.

### Types

| Type | Fiction |
|---|---|
| **Walker** | The overspill. Ordinary people the exposure simply killed and restarted. The majority. |
| **Runner** | Fresher, or a stronger dose. Still fast because the body has not wrecked itself yet. |
| **Ghost** | The trials that *worked*. Coherent, directed, and wrong — they move as though walls are a formality and they are never hard to see, because they are not hiding. These are the program's successes. |
| **Brute** | Long exposure at Ashworks. Bodies that kept accumulating mass instead of failing. |
| **Carrier** | Not an attacker. A piece of the Slagmaw that detached and is trying to get home. Deals no damage; it exists to feed. |

## Patient Furnace

**One creature.** `Slagmaw` is the internal/code name inherited from Pixel Horde
and never appears in player-facing text. In fiction it is only ever *Patient
Furnace*, or whatever the survivors call it.

Central Quarantine was both the command post and the disposal site. That is the
most damning fact in the game and it needs no document to explain: the people
running the containment burned the bodies in the same complex they filed the
paperwork in. The pile never finished burning. It kept being added to. It got up.

**Why the survivors call it that.** They do not name it. They read it, off a
containment placard at the arena entrance, printed as two fields on a form:

```
SUBJECT ...... [REDACTED]
DISPOSITION .. FURNACE
```

The name is a filing decision that turned into a monster. Keep it clinical and
industrial — never fantasy. It is a case designation, not a boss title.

**Why the carriers heal it.** It is made of bodies, so it can be fed bodies.
The carriers are its own shed mass crawling back. Ideal extension: let carriers
retrieve infected corpses from the arena floor, so the squad's own kills become
the boss's health pool.

## The objective chain

Players spawn at **76, 2705** — inside the gate at Checkpoint Nine. Escaping is
the goal; everything else is what escaping costs. The world remains open to
scouting and backtracking, but the actions themselves have physical dependencies.
Return trips are load-bearing: they are where the squad scavenges, levels, and
earns the fight, and accumulating scent means the road back is never the road
they came in on.

**Act I — South Blocks. Prepare.**
The crossroads market is stripped in public and unevenly missed in private:
locked stock, a loading cage, and small overlooked reserves make it the first
expedition rather than a solution. Evacuation signs and requisition paperwork
point separately toward Ashworks fuel, the Old Quarter refuge, St. Orison,
Central Quarantine, and Blackglass.

**Act II — Ashworks. Recover fuel.**
The emergency network needs machine fuel, and Ashworks is where the city stored
it. The machine-shop compound also holds an operable bulldozer. The machine can
open shortcuts and supply approaches, but no required destination depends on it.

**Act III — Old Quarter. Restore the refuge circuit.**
The chapel was a first-response refuge. Its generator feeds the surviving
emergency circuit shared by Blackglass and Checkpoint Nine. Restoring it makes
later actions possible; bedrolls, notices, and abandoned supplies show what the
refuge was without needing a survivor to explain it.

**Act IV — Civic Ward. Read what St. Orison left behind.**
The hospital records connect the civilian trials to Patient Furnace and point to
the command payload and gate override inside Central Quarantine. Players can
enter the ward earlier, but this evidence makes the next dependency legible.

**Act V — Central Quarantine. Kill Patient Furnace.**
The command post and disposal site are the same compound. Killing the local
attractor reduces the pull on the city and exposes the command payload: the open
band, the evidence of what happened, and the override for Checkpoint Nine.

**Act VI — Northline. Transmit and hold Blackglass.**
With emergency power and the command payload, the squad can broadcast the truth
and a distress call. A civilian voice may answer and confirm a rendezvous outside
Checkpoint Nine, but no rescue force, supplies, or outside operator arrives. The
signal tells people where survival may still be possible; it does not perform the
escape.

**Act VII — Checkpoint Nine. Open it and get everyone out.**
The squad returns south with nine days of accumulated scent behind it, uses the
restored circuit and recovered override, and opens the gate itself. The people
waiting inside move only because the protagonists made the route and the exit.

## Districts

The story landmark is what the chapter remembers; the urban anchor is what
organizes the surrounding streets, services, interiors, and loot. They can be
different places in the same district.

| District | Story landmark | Urban anchor | Role in the fiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **South Blocks** | Checkpoint Nine | Crossroads supermarket | Where quarantine sealed the city. Start and end; the market supports the first expedition. |
| **Northline** | Blackglass Radio | Broadcast station and tower | The severed link and final warning beacon. Act VI. |
| **Central Quarantine** | Patient Furnace | Command/incinerator compound | Military processing, clinical holding, and disposal occupy the same site. Act V. |
| **Civic Ward** | St. Orison Hospital | Hospital campus | Where the trials were run. It reads as a medical site before it reads as a combat zone. Act IV. |
| **Ashworks** | Furnace Plant | Machine-shop compound | Fuel and heavy industry; overflow was burned here when the incinerator could not keep up. Act II. |
| **Old Quarter** | The Collapsed Quarter | Graveyard and refuge chapel | Demolition closed streets; the refuge generator keeps the surviving emergency circuit possible. Act III. |

## Writing rules

1. **Bureaucratic horror, not monster horror.** The worst thing in the game is
   that someone processed this. Forms, stamps, manifests, disposition fields.
2. **Nobody narrates.** Civilians do not have the vocabulary. The player assembles
   the story; the squad never states it.
3. **Paper over voice.** Day 9 means every notice is still readable. Use them.
4. **The government is absent, not present.** No living soldiers, no radio orders,
   and no arriving rescue. Only what they left and what they sealed.
5. **Clinical + industrial.** Where those two registers collide is the game's voice.
